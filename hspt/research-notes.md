# HSPT format notes

Short notes for this practice app. Checked November 20, 2026. This app is not affiliated with Scholastic Testing Service, and it does not use official test questions.

## What the test is

The High School Placement Test (HSPT) is published by Scholastic Testing Service (STS). Catholic high schools use it for admission, scholarships, and ninth-grade placement. The grade 8 form is the HSPT. A grade 7 form is called the Pre-HSPT. STS still describes the test as paper-and-pencil (an online answer sheet is optional). Testing time for the five-part battery is about 2 hours 30 minutes. Schools often schedule about 3 hours so they can pass out booklets and give short breaks.

Sources:

- STS product page: https://www.stsme.com/prod/hspt/
- STS parent page: https://ststesting.com/hspt/
- STS interpretation guide (PDF): https://www.ststesting.com/hp_int_sts.pdf

## Five sections

STS says the battery has five multiple-choice subtests and is meant to be taken as a whole.

| Section | Items | Time limit used in this app | What it measures (STS wording) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verbal Skills | 60 | 16 minutes | Synonyms, antonyms, verbal analogies, verbal classification, logical reasoning |
| Quantitative Skills | 52 | 30 minutes | Number series, number manipulations, geometric and nongeometric comparisons. No calculator. |
| Reading | 62 | 25 minutes | Main ideas, details, purpose, inference, literary technique, vocabulary in context |
| Mathematics | 64 | 45 minutes | Number, measurement, geometry, algebra, statistics. Computation and problem solving. No calculator. |
| Language | 60 | 25 minutes | Punctuation, capitalization, spelling, grammar, usage, composition |

Total: 60 + 52 + 62 + 64 + 60 = **298 questions**. The five time limits add to **141 minutes** (2 hours 21 minutes). The “about 2.5 hours” figure is the battery itself; a school day often runs longer because of directions and breaks.

STS’s interpretation guide states the item counts above. It does not print the minutes. The same 16 / 30 / 25 / 45 / 25 minute limits are published by the Archdiocese of Washington Catholic Schools, which uses the STS test:

https://adwcatholicschools.org/high-school/placement-tests/about-hspt/

That page also says the exam is 298 questions and that a full sitting is about 3 hours including two short breaks and about 30 minutes for materials. A parent handout from a Catholic school in Memphis lists the same counts and times:

https://stlouismemphis.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/High-School-Placement-Test-Information.pdf

STS does **not** publish how many synonyms versus analogies are inside the 60 verbal items, or the exact mix inside the other sections. This app uses a balanced mix and says so on the mock-test screen.

Most items have four choices, A–D. Quantitative comparisons on the real test are often three choices (A is greater, B is greater, or they are equal). This app follows that.

An optional 40-item Science or Catholic Religion test can be given after the battery. It is not part of the composite score. This app does not include it.

## Scoring

From the STS parent page and the interpretation guide:

- Raw score = number correct. Wrong answers do not subtract points. Guessing is not punished.
- Each raw score is turned into a **standard score** from **200 to 800**. The mean is **500** and the standard deviation is **100**. Scales are equated across forms, so they are not a simple percent.
- **National percentile** (1–99) compares a student with the national norm group. A percentile of 64 means the student scored higher than 64 percent of that group. Percentiles are not equal intervals.
- **Local percentile** compares a student with the local testing group.
- Reports also include stanines and grade equivalents.
- Composites are scaled on their own. They are not the average of the parts.
  - Total Cognitive Skills: Verbal + Quantitative. Schools sometimes also see a Cognitive Skills Quotient with an average near 100.
  - Total Basic Skills: Reading + Mathematics + Language.
  - Battery Composite: all five subtests.

The Archdiocese of Washington page describes the same 200–800 standard scores, with 500 as the mean.

This app shows a raw score and an **approximate** score on a 200–800 line (percent correct mapped in a straight line). That is not an STS equated score and not a national percentile. The results page says so.

## Other rules that matter for practice

- No calculator, phone, or other device.
- Scratch work is allowed.
- The proctor says when half the time is gone.
- Answer every item.

Cruz’s test date for this app is January 9, 2027. That date is a family deadline for practice, not an STS national date.
